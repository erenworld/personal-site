#### Pick one company and go deep

Don't send the same application to fifty companies. Choose the one you really want to join and learn everything you can about it. Read their engineering blog and see what they are working on right now. For example, on the Cloudflare blog, you can see how they solve problems.  
[How we found a bug in Go’s ARM64 compiler](https://blog.cloudflare.com/how-we-found-a-bug-in-gos-arm64-compiler/)  

Each engineer writes about their approach to solving a problem. Contact those engineers. Solve problems for them. Improve their open-source codebase. Make a pull request. Don’t use AI. Show your real skills.

#### Make your interest obvious

Don't wait for a job posting. Write to them directly and tell them, in your own words, why them and not another company. Then come with something concrete: a bug you fixed, an idea for their product, a pull request you opened. Saying you want it is not enough, you have to show it.

#### Be Extraordinary

Resumes are boring. What really stands out is when someone is extraordinary.  
Build a personal brand in a very specific niche. Be like an expert in the field. Post daily on X. Build side projects that show what you are capable of.

Some examples of extraordinary people:
- [Ben Dicken](https://benjdd.com/)
- [Fatih Arslan](https://arslan.io/about/)
- [Dmitrii Kovanikov](https://chshersh.com/)
- [Lee Rob](https://leerob.com/)

Look at what they write, what they tweet, how they think, which projects they made, look at their commits, analyze them like a scientist.

#### Follow up, and keep it short

One message is easy to miss. Follow up regularly: an email, a message on LinkedIn, a quick chat at a meetup or a conference. Each time, get to the point fast and bring something new, like a fresh contribution or a better question. Being a bit insistent is fine. Being forgotten is worse.

#### Send 10 cold emails per day

Don’t underestimate cold emails. This is the most powerful tool to get your dream job.  
If you know how to write emails and get people’s attention, you’ll get it. I put together [some templates and resources](https://www.notion.so/email-2eaabe1bd3f8800f9076da4f3c2ed142) on Notion to help you.

Keep this quote in mind:

> Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you: For every one that asketh receiveth; and he that seeketh findeth; and to him that knocketh it shall be opened

#### Don't define yourself by a language

"JavaScript developer" or "backend developer" describes a tool, not what you bring. Companies don't hire you to write code. They hire you because something in their business needs to get better: more revenue, lower costs, fewer problems.

So talk about results. What did you ship, what did it change, who did it help? If you don't have work experience yet, show it with your projects, or come with ideas.

#### You can network from your bedroom

For me, networking just means talking to people you can learn from, and maybe helping them back. No suit, no business club. LinkedIn and X are enough. Write to people whose work you admire.

Something like this works:

> Hi X, I came across your GitHub profile, and I find it really inspiring that someone from Metz is a major contributor to Postgres. I’d love to get your advice on how to become a database specialist. What resources, books, or projects helped you the most? It would be great to chat with you sometime.

#### Communication matters as much as code

Being good technically is not enough on its own. People need to understand what you can do and believe you will make a difference. A great engineer who can't explain their work is easy to overlook. Practice explaining what you do, simply and quickly, even to someone who isn't technical.

Look at how Robin Malfait got hired at Tailwind: [From 900 to 1: how we hired Robin Malfait](https://tailwindcss.com/blog/from-900-to-1-how-we-hired-robin-malfait).
